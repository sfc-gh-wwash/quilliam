Below is a concise recap of the Snowflake and Accenture discussion on the Vertex CoCo demo and joint go-to-market approach.

### Overall Summary

The meeting focused on two related topics: Snowflake’s demo of Cortex Code (referred to in the meeting as CoCo) for Vertex use cases, and Accenture’s presentation of CoCo Builder as a joint accelerator. The most important outcome was alignment that both capabilities could support a broader co-sell motion into Vertex, with the teams planning to regroup next week to discuss account strategy and concrete use cases.

### Meeting Flow

- Snowflake opened by framing the session around three goals: demonstrate a custom Vertex-oriented Snowflake/Cortex Code use case, introduce Accenture’s joint CoCo Builder accelerator, and discuss how Snowflake and Accenture could coordinate a co-sell motion into the Vertex account.
- **William Wash** walked through a demo built around Vertex’s stated need for natural-language querying over customer tax data in a multi-tenant Snowflake environment. He emphasized two technical problems being addressed: enforcing tenant-specific authorization and row-level security, and supplementing Snowflake semantic models with metadata discovery from Atlan when needed.
- The demo showed how Cortex Code can generate a realistic data model, apply governance controls, create a React interface, and support tenant-aware querying through Cortex Agents. The group discussed how built-in Snowflake skills can be copied and customized for customer-specific governance patterns.
- The conversation then shifted to platform and compliance questions. **William Wash** said Snowflake supports certifications such as SOC 2 at the platform level and pointed attendees to trust.snowflake.com for reports. The team also discussed additional uses for Cortex Code, including performance monitoring, query optimization, automated Jira ticket creation, and migration work from legacy platforms.
- Accenture asked how CoCo could work with its broader AMS-oriented agentic architecture. **William Wash** said headless and CLI-driven use cases are possible today, especially for script generation and data interaction, while fuller agent-to-agent patterns are not yet available but are expected in private preview soon.
- In the second half, Accenture presented CoCo Builder as a UI-driven accelerator that can turn business requirements into prototype agentic solutions through a gated, human-reviewed pipeline. The positioning was that it rapidly creates working prototypes, not production-ready assets, and could help delivery teams stand up use cases in hours or days rather than weeks.
